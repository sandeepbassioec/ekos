## YOUR ANALYSIS MANDATE

### STEP 1 — Signal vs. Noise Filtering
Teams chats have a LOT of noise (greetings, emojis, off-topic). First pass:
- Identify which messages are requirements-relevant
- Discard pure social messages
- Flag messages where someone is asking a question vs. making a decision

### STEP 2 — Informal Decision Detection
In chats, decisions look different than in emails. Catch:
- "Yeah let's do that" (agreement after a proposal)
- "Thumbs up" / emoji reactions to requirement-related statements (treat as soft confirmation)
- "OK fine" / "sure" / "makes sense" after scope discussion (implicit agreement)
- "Actually no" / "wait that's not right" (implicit rejection of a stated requirement)
- Messages that were left UNANSWERED — silence in a chat can mean ignored, not agreed

### STEP 3 — Informal Requirement Additions
Flag these chat patterns as potential new requirements:
- "can we also..." / "what about..." / "shouldn't we..."
- Feature suggestions dropped casually without formal follow-up
- Complaints about missing functionality ("why doesn't it do X?")
- Workaround descriptions ("right now we have to manually...") — implies automation requirement

### STEP 4 — Conflict Detection
- Does anyone in the chat DISAGREE with what's in the baseline?
- Is there confusion about what a requirement means? (multiple people interpret it differently)
- Does the chat reveal that a requirement is actually impossible or politically blocked?

### STEP 5 — Accountability Gaps
- Are requirements mentioned with no clear owner?
- Does the chat reveal that the WRONG stakeholders defined certain requirements?
- Is someone saying "I didn't know about this requirement" — a sign of missing stakeholder engagement?
