## SA-01 — Solution Intake & Brief {#sa-01}

### SA-01-A: Brief Capture & Classification

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA01-001 | Solution Brief Wizard | The suite SHALL provide a structured, multi-step intake wizard that captures solution name, sponsoring business domain, sponsoring stakeholder, problem statement, desired business outcomes, estimated strategic value, requested delivery timeline, and linked enterprise capability from the EA capability map. | Functional | P2 | Must | Without a canonical brief, downstream scope, options, and decisions have no shared anchor; gaps arise late when changes are costly. |
| REQ-SA01-002 | Multi-format Brief Ingestion | The suite SHALL accept a solution brief in free-text, uploaded document (Word/PDF/email/chat transcript), or structured form, and SHALL extract structured fields using the AI extraction pipeline (Volume 07) populating the brief wizard automatically with confidence scores. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | SAs frequently receive briefs as emails or slide decks; manual re-entry wastes time and introduces errors. |
| REQ-SA01-003 | Brief Template Library | The suite SHALL provide a minimum of eight configurable brief templates differentiated by solution category (e.g., net-new application, platform migration, API-first product, data & analytics platform, SaaS adoption, integration programme, infrastructure modernisation, and security uplift), each pre-populating relevant fields and guiding questions. | Functional | P2 | Must | Template-guided capture reduces blank-slate friction and ensures domain-appropriate questions are asked. |
| REQ-SA01-004 | Stakeholder Register on Brief | The brief SHALL capture a stakeholder register with name, organisation unit, role relative to the solution (sponsor, decision maker, subject matter expert, affected user, external party), and communication preference; each stakeholder SHALL be linkable to constraints, requirements, and decisions later in the solution lifecycle. | Functional | P2 | Must | Stakeholder accountability is a TOGAF ADM prerequisite (Phase A); missing it breaks sign-off traceability. |
| REQ-SA01-005 | Business Context Linkage | The suite SHALL allow the brief to be linked to one or more enterprise strategic initiatives, business capabilities, value streams, and portfolio items from the EA layer (V01), so that the solution scope is always grounded in enterprise context. | Integration | P1, P2 | Must | Prevents solutions from being funded without enterprise strategic alignment. |
| REQ-SA01-006 | Brief Versioning | Every edit to a brief SHALL create a new immutable version with timestamp, author, and a diff of changed fields, so that the evolution of scope and objectives is fully auditable. | Governance | P2 | Must | Scope creep most often begins with undocumented verbal changes to the initial brief. |
| REQ-SA01-007 | Brief Completeness Scoring | The suite SHALL compute a completeness score (0–100 %) for each brief based on mandatory and recommended fields; SAs SHALL be alerted to critical gaps (e.g., missing business outcomes, missing strategic alignment) before proceeding to options generation. | UX | P2 | Should | Partial briefs produce poorly scoped options and lead to rework downstream. |
| REQ-SA01-008 | Brief Status Workflow | The brief SHALL follow a configurable status lifecycle (Draft → Under Review → Approved → In Design → Superseded) with status-appropriate actions and notifications to the sponsor and designated reviewers. | Functional | P2 | Must | Prevents work beginning on unapproved or contested briefs. |
| REQ-SA01-009 | AI Brief Summary | The suite SHALL generate a concise executive summary (≤ 300 words) of the brief suitable for sponsor communication, preserving the stated problem, objectives, and out-of-scope statements, with one-click export to Word or email. | AI/ML | P2 | Should | SAs routinely spend 30–60 minutes writing stakeholder-facing summaries from raw notes. |
| REQ-SA01-010 | Out-of-Scope Declaration | The brief SHALL include an explicit out-of-scope section where the SA records what is intentionally excluded; the suite SHALL surface this at every gate review and in the final solution document. | Functional | P2 | Must | Scope exclusions prevent re-opening of settled decisions and protect delivery commitments. |
| REQ-SA01-011 | Predecessor Solution Linkage | The suite SHALL allow a brief to reference a predecessor or superseded solution, carrying forward its ADRs, RAID register, and relevant building blocks as optional starting-point inheritance. | Functional | P2 | Should | Reuse of prior solution context reduces ramp-up time and avoids repeating past mistakes. |
| REQ-SA01-012 | Brief Collaboration | Multiple users with edit or comment permissions SHALL be able to annotate, question, or suggest changes to brief fields in real time; unresolved comments SHALL block brief approval. | UX | P2 | Should | Distributed stakeholders co-author briefs; asynchronous review is the norm. |

## SA-02 — Requirements-to-Scope Mapping {#sa-02}

### SA-02-A: Requirements Import & Linkage

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA02-001 | Requirements Pull from REQM Pillar | The suite SHALL allow a solution to pull a curated set of requirements from the Requirements pillar (V07), selecting by project, baseline, classification (Functional/NFR/Constraint), status, and priority; pulled requirements SHALL be versioned-linked so changes in V07 propagate as change-requests to the solution. | Integration | P2 | Must | SA scope is built from requirements; disconnected copies lead to drift. |
| REQ-SA02-002 | In-context Requirements Authoring | SAs SHALL be able to author solution-level requirements directly within the solution workspace if a V07 project does not yet exist, with those requirements later promotable to the REQM pillar. | Functional | P2 | Must | Some solutions begin in SA before formal requirements management is set up. |
| REQ-SA02-003 | Scope Boundary Canvas | The suite SHALL provide a visual scope boundary canvas (analogous to a C4 context diagram) where the SA assigns each requirement to one of: In-Scope, Out-of-Scope, Deferred, Dependency-on-Third-Party, or Assumed-in-Environment; each assignment SHALL be capturable as a rationale statement. | UX | P2 | Must | Visual scope declaration reduces handover ambiguity and is a prerequisite for HLD generation. |
| REQ-SA02-004 | NFR Extraction to NFR Register | The suite SHALL automatically identify and extract all non-functional requirements from the imported or authored requirements set and pre-populate the solution's NFR register (SA-09), flagging NFRs without quantified targets for SA completion. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | Unquantified NFRs are the most common root cause of architecture under- or over-engineering. |
| REQ-SA02-005 | Constraint Extraction | The suite SHALL extract technology, organisational, regulatory, budget, and timeline constraints from requirements text and surface them in the solution's RAID register (SA-15) under the Constraints section. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | Constraints must be visible before options are generated; otherwise invalid options are proposed and effort is wasted. |
| REQ-SA02-006 | Requirements Coverage Matrix | The suite SHALL maintain a live requirements coverage matrix mapping each requirement to the solution components, ADRs, HLD sections, and LLD artefacts that address it, so that SA and delivery teams can see at any time which requirements are addressed, partially addressed, or unaddressed. | Reporting | P2 | Must | Coverage matrices are mandatory deliverables at most enterprise architecture governance gates. |
| REQ-SA02-007 | Conflict & Tension Detection | The AI engine SHALL scan the linked requirements set and surface conflicts (two requirements that contradict each other), tensions (two requirements that are difficult to satisfy simultaneously at given targets), and duplicates, annotating each with severity and suggested resolution. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | Unresolved requirement conflicts are a leading cause of design rework and go-live defects. |
| REQ-SA02-008 | Scope Change Impact Analysis | When a requirement's status, priority, or content changes after scope lock, the suite SHALL compute the impact on linked solution components, ADRs, NFR targets, cost estimates, and the RAID register, presenting a change impact summary for SA sign-off. | Functional | P2 | Must | Uncontrolled scope changes without impact assessment are the primary driver of cost and schedule overrun. |
| REQ-SA02-009 | Assumption Surfacing | The suite SHALL allow SAs to record explicit solution assumptions (statements believed true but not yet verified) against any requirement or scope item, and SHALL track assumption status (Unvalidated / Validated / Refuted), triggering risk entries for refuted assumptions. | Functional | P2 | Must | Unstated assumptions are latent risks; making them explicit enables proactive management. |
| REQ-SA02-010 | Scope Baseline Snapshot | The SA SHALL be able to take a named, immutable scope baseline snapshot at any point in the lifecycle (e.g., at HLD approval); the snapshot captures all in-scope requirements, their versions, NFR targets, and scope boundary assignments. | Governance | P2 | Must | Baseline snapshots are the reference for change control and deviation analysis. |

## SA-03 — Solution Options Generation {#sa-03}

### SA-03-A: Options Framing

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA03-001 | Option Set Framework | The suite SHALL generate a minimum of three solution options for any solution brief: Option 0 (Do-Nothing / Baseline), at least one Minimal Viable option, and at least one Target/Strategic option; additional options SHALL be generatable on demand. | Functional | P2 | Must | Single-option presentations limit stakeholder agency and prevent informed decision-making. |
| REQ-SA03-002 | AI-Assisted Options Generation | The AI engine SHALL propose solution options using the solution type, NFR targets, technology preferences, identified constraints, and matching reference architectures and Solution Building Blocks from the catalog; each proposed option SHALL include a brief description, primary pattern, key components, and indicative risk/cost/time classification. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | Manual options generation from scratch is time-intensive; AI acceleration reduces SA effort by 50–70 %. |
| REQ-SA03-003 | Build-vs-Buy-vs-Reuse Options | For any solution involving a product, platform, or capability that may be satisfied by a commercial product, SaaS offering, or existing internal system, the suite SHALL generate explicit Build, Buy, Reuse, and Hybrid option variants, pre-populating vendor candidates from the technology catalog (SA-12). | Functional | P2 | Must | Build-vs-buy analysis is a mandatory deliverable for most governance processes and procurement policies. |
| REQ-SA03-004 | Option Customisation | SAs SHALL be able to edit, rename, clone, merge, or delete generated options; all modifications SHALL be versioned and attributed. | UX | P2 | Must | AI-generated options are starting points; SA expertise shapes the final option set. |
| REQ-SA03-005 | Option Description Template | Each option SHALL capture: Name, Type (Build/Buy/Reuse/Hybrid), Description, Key Architectural Pattern, Primary Technologies, Integration Approach, Data Approach, Deployment Model, Team/Skill Requirements, and Indicative Delivery Timeline. | Functional | P2 | Must | Consistent option descriptions enable like-for-like comparison and prevent partial analysis. |
| REQ-SA03-006 | Options Linkage to Reference Architectures | Each generated option SHALL be linkable to one or more reference architectures or Solution Building Blocks from the catalog (SA-06) so that the reuse chain is explicit and audit-ready. | Governance | P2 | Should | Demonstrating reference architecture reuse is a key enterprise architecture compliance indicator. |
| REQ-SA03-007 | Option Diagram Auto-generation | For each option the suite SHALL auto-generate a high-level context/container diagram (C4 Level 1–2 or ArchiMate application layer view) illustrating key components, integrations, and deployment targets, using the diagramming engine (V03). | Functional | P2 | Must | Visual representations of options are essential for communicating with non-technical stakeholders. |
| REQ-SA03-008 | PoC / Spike Planning for Options | For options with significant unknowns the suite SHALL generate a PoC or spike plan template including: objective, hypothesis, success criteria, timebox, required skills, and expected artefacts, so the option can be de-risked before a final recommendation. | Functional | P2 | Should | Unverified high-risk options often survive options analysis because no structured PoC was planned. |
| REQ-SA03-009 | Sensitivity to Constraint Changes | The suite SHALL model how the option ranking changes if key constraints are relaxed or tightened (e.g., budget increased by 20 %, deadline extended by 3 months), providing a sensitivity summary alongside each option. | Functional | P2 | Could | Constraint sensitivity awareness enables better stakeholder negotiation. |

## SA-04 — Trade-off & Decision Analysis {#sa-04}

### SA-04-A: Weighted Scoring & Analysis Tools

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA04-001 | Configurable Weighted Scoring Matrix | The suite SHALL provide a weighted scoring matrix where the SA defines evaluation criteria (e.g., Cost, Risk, Delivery Speed, Alignment to Strategy, Technical Fit, Vendor Support, Maintainability, Security), assigns weights (summing to 100), scores each option per criterion (1–5 or 1–10), and the suite computes the weighted total automatically. | Functional | P2 | Must | Weighted scoring is the most widely used formal trade-off technique in enterprise SA practice; absence forces subjective opinions to masquerade as analysis. |
| REQ-SA04-002 | Criteria Library | The suite SHALL provide a library of ≥ 50 pre-defined evaluation criteria with descriptions and typical weight ranges, organised by category (Financial, Technical, Operational, Strategic, Risk, Regulatory); SAs SHALL be able to create custom criteria. | Functional | P2 | Should | A criteria library reduces blank-canvas paralysis and ensures important criteria are not inadvertently omitted. |
| REQ-SA04-003 | Pros/Cons Capture per Option | Each option SHALL have a structured pros/cons panel where the SA records free-text advantages, disadvantages, and mitigations; the suite SHALL allow AI-assist to generate initial pros/cons from the option description. | Functional | P2 | Must | Pros/cons panels are the most common trade-off communication format for executive audiences. |
| REQ-SA04-004 | Cost vs Risk vs Time Plot | The suite SHALL render a 2D or 3D scatter plot of options across Cost (x), Risk (y), and Time-to-Deliver (z or bubble size) dimensions using the indicative values captured per option, allowing interactive comparison. | UX | P2 | Should | Visual cost-risk-time plots communicate option trade-offs to executives more effectively than tables. |
| REQ-SA04-005 | Multi-stakeholder Scoring | The suite SHALL allow multiple stakeholders (e.g., SA, business sponsor, security architect, finance) to independently score the same matrix; individual scores SHALL be visible alongside the aggregate, enabling the SA to identify and facilitate disagreements. | Functional | P2 | Should | Distributed scoring reveals hidden stakeholder disagreements before a decision is finalised. |
| REQ-SA04-006 | Recommendation Statement Generation | After scoring, the suite SHALL generate a recommended option statement (≤ 200 words) that cites the score, top-weighted criteria favouring the recommendation, and the key risk mitigation required, ready for inclusion in the decision document. | AI/ML | P2 | Should | SAs spend significant time writing recommendation summaries; AI generation based on structured data reduces this to editing. |
| REQ-SA04-007 | Sensitivity Analysis on Weights | The suite SHALL compute how the final recommendation changes as individual criterion weights are varied by ±10 % and ±20 %, and SHALL identify criteria whose weight changes flip the recommendation, flagging these as decision leverage points. | Functional | P2 | Should | Weight sensitivity analysis ensures the recommendation is robust to reasonable disagreements on weighting. |
| REQ-SA04-008 | Qualitative Risk Assessment per Option | For each option the suite SHALL capture a qualitative risk assessment covering technical risk, delivery risk, vendor/supply risk, regulatory/compliance risk, and organisational change risk, each scored (Low/Medium/High/Critical) with rationale, and aggregated into a risk profile. | Functional | P2 | Must | Risk profiling per option is required by most enterprise governance frameworks before a decision can be approved. |
| REQ-SA04-009 | Decision Audit Trail | Every score, weight, comment, and recommendation in the trade-off analysis SHALL be immutably logged with timestamp and user, so the decision is fully reconstructable for post-project review. | Governance | P2 | Must | Audit trails for architectural decisions are required by ISO/IEC 25010, TOGAF, and most regulated-industry governance frameworks. |
| REQ-SA04-010 | Export to Decision Paper | The complete options analysis (options, matrix, pros/cons, recommendation) SHALL be exportable as a structured decision paper in Word/PDF format, using the deliverable templates defined in V10. | Reporting | P2 | Must | Governance gates require a decision paper; manual formatting of analysis outputs is a common SA time sink. |

## SA-05 — Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) & Decision Logs {#sa-05}

### SA-05-A: ADR Lifecycle Management

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA05-001 | ADR Authoring Interface | The suite SHALL provide a structured ADR authoring interface with the fields: ID (auto-generated, prefix configurable), Title, Status, Date, Authors, Deciders, Context & Problem Statement, Decision Drivers, Considered Options, Decision Outcome, Positive Consequences, Negative Consequences, and Confirmation/Compliance Method. | Functional | P2 | Must | ADR format is widely standardised (Michael Nygard / MADR / RFC-style); a structured interface prevents incomplete records. |
| REQ-SA05-002 | ADR Status Lifecycle | ADR status SHALL follow a configurable workflow: Proposed → Under Review → Accepted → Deprecated → Superseded; status transitions SHALL require an authorised actor and may require comments or linking to a superseding ADR. | Governance | P2 | Must | Status-less ADRs cannot be relied upon by downstream consumers; a team does not know whether a decision is still valid. |
| REQ-SA05-003 | ADR–Option Linkage | Each ADR SHALL be linkable to the solution option it was made within, the specific trade-off criteria it resolved, and the requirements or constraints that drove it. | Functional | P2 | Must | Traceable linkage from requirement → constraint → option analysis → ADR is required for impact analysis and change management. |
| REQ-SA05-004 | ADR Supersession Chain | When an ADR is superseded the suite SHALL require the author to link the new superseding ADR, capture the reason for supersession, and SHALL display the supersession chain (ordered history of related decisions on the same topic) in a dedicated view. | Governance | P2 | Must | Without explicit supersession tracking, stale ADRs continue to be referenced in delivery, causing defects. |
| REQ-SA05-005 | Decision Log Summary View | The suite SHALL provide a decision log view at the solution level listing all ADRs with ID, title, status, date, authors, and brief outcome summary, filterable by status, author, topic, linked component, and date range. | Functional | P2 | Must | Decision logs are standard deliverables for design reviews; a consolidated log view is required by stakeholders who need to review all decisions for a solution. |
| REQ-SA05-006 | AI-Assisted ADR Drafting | Given a chosen option and the trade-off analysis, the AI engine SHALL draft an ADR skeleton pre-populating Context, Considered Options, Decision Outcome, and Consequences fields, which the SA then refines. | AI/ML | P2 | Should | ADR authoring is a consistently time-consuming task; AI first-draft reduces elapsed time by 40–60 %. |
| REQ-SA05-007 | ADR Cross-Solution Reuse | ADRs marked as Reusable SHALL be discoverable across the ADR library and usable as starting-point templates or read-only references in new solutions, supporting enterprise decision knowledge reuse. | Functional | P2 | Should | Many architectural decisions (e.g., "use JWT for service-to-service auth") recur across solutions; forcing re-analysis increases cost and introduces inconsistency. |
| REQ-SA05-008 | ADR Dependency Graph | The suite SHALL render a dependency graph of ADRs within a solution showing which ADRs depend on, reference, or supersede each other, enabling impact analysis when a decision changes. | UX | P2 | Could | In complex solutions with 20+ ADRs, inter-decision dependencies are non-obvious and impact analysis without a graph is error-prone. |
| REQ-SA05-009 | ADR Compliance Tagging | Each ADR SHALL be taggable with enterprise standards, principles, or policies it complies with or deviates from; deviations SHALL automatically generate a waiver request entry (SA-18). | Governance | P2 | Must | Demonstrating standards compliance at the decision level is required in regulated industries. |
| REQ-SA05-010 | ADR Notification & Review | When an ADR is created or changes status the suite SHALL notify configured reviewers and shall support inline comment threads anchored to specific ADR fields; all comments SHALL be archived with the ADR. | UX | P2 | Should | Asynchronous review is the norm in distributed SA teams; in-context commenting reduces email volume. |
| REQ-SA05-011 | ADR Export & Publication | Individual ADRs and the complete decision log SHALL be exportable to Markdown, Word, PDF, and Confluence page formats; export SHALL preserve all fields, linkage references, and status history. | Reporting | P2 | Must | ADRs are routinely published to Confluence, GitHub, or project documentation portals; manual formatting is a friction point. |
| REQ-SA05-012 | ADR Search & Discovery | A full-text and faceted search over all ADRs (across solutions and programmes) SHALL be available, supporting search by keyword, technology, component, standard, decision outcome, and date range. | Functional | P2 | Must | Without search, ADR knowledge silos within solutions and is not reused enterprise-wide. |

| REQ-SA06-005 | Reference Architecture Search & Recommendation | When a SA defines a solution type, NFR targets, deployment model, and integration profile, the suite SHALL recommend the top-3 most applicable reference architectures from the catalog with a match score and explanation. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | Discovery of the right reference architecture in a large catalog without recommendation requires manual browsing; recommendation reduces time-to-design significantly. |
| REQ-SA06-006 | Community and Enterprise Submissions | Authorised users SHALL be able to submit new reference architectures or variants for review; the submission SHALL follow an approval workflow (Draft → Review → Approved) with comments and required fields. | Governance | P2 | Should | Reference architecture catalogs that only central teams can update become stale and unused. |

| REQ-SA06-013 | SBB Retirement & Replacement | When an SBB is deprecated or retired the suite SHALL identify all active solutions using it, generate a migration impact summary per solution, and notify the respective SAs with a recommended replacement SBB and migration guidance. | Governance | P2 | Must | Unmanaged SBB retirement leaves solutions on unsupported, insecure, or end-of-life components. |

## SA-07 — High-Level Design (HLD) Automation {#sa-07}

> Note: ESIP today has HLD generator stubs. This section specifies the full vision for AI-driven, structured HLD generation.

### SA-07-A: HLD Structure & Content

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA07-001 | Structured HLD Document Model | The suite SHALL generate an HLD that is a structured document model (not a flat text file) with named, individually versioned sections: Executive Summary, Solution Context, Key Decisions (linked ADRs), Architecture Overview, Component Architecture, Integration Architecture, Data Architecture, Security Architecture, Infrastructure Architecture, NFR Targets & Design Approaches, Risks & Assumptions, and Open Issues. | Functional | P2 | Must | Structured HLDs allow section-level diff, comment threads, status per section, and partial regeneration without overwriting SA edits. |
| REQ-SA07-002 | HLD Auto-generation from Solution Inputs | The suite SHALL auto-generate a complete HLD first draft from the solution brief, scoped requirements, chosen option, selected SBBs, reference architecture, NFR targets, integration profile, and RAID register without requiring SA to author content from scratch. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | AI-driven first drafts reduce HLD elapsed time from days to hours; the SA role shifts from authoring to reviewing and refining. |

| REQ-SA13-007 | Anti-Corruption Layer Identification | Where the solution integrates with a legacy system or third-party system with a substantially different domain model, the AI engine SHALL flag the integration as requiring anti-corruption layer (ACL) consideration and SHALL recommend the appropriate translation pattern. | AI/ML | P2 | Should | Missing ACLs cause domain model pollution that leads to long-term maintainability defects. |

## SA-14 — Data Design at Solution Level {#sa-14}

> Boundary: Conceptual/logical/physical data model governance, data lineage, and enterprise data catalog are V05 (Data Architect). This section covers the SA's coordination and solution-level data design responsibilities.

### SA-14-A: Solution Data Architecture

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA14-001 | Data Store Inventory | The solution SHALL maintain a data store inventory listing every data store (RDBMS, NoSQL, cache, object store, data warehouse, lake, event log) with: store name, technology, deployment target, data classification (Public/Internal/Confidential/Restricted), owner, retention policy, backup policy, and linkage to the solution ER/data model. | Data | P2 | Must | Data store inventory is required for security classification, compliance, and operational readiness assessments. |
| REQ-SA14-002 | Data Flow Diagram | The suite SHALL generate a Data Flow Diagram (DFD) at solution level showing how data moves between solution components, external systems, and data stores, with data type annotations, transformation points, and classification labels. | Functional | P2 | Must | DFDs are required by data protection officers, security architects, and compliance teams to assess data handling practices. |
| REQ-SA14-003 | Data Classification Propagation | When a data field is classified as Confidential or Restricted in the data model, the suite SHALL propagate that classification to all APIs, event schemas, and integration contracts that transmit the field, generating compliance requirements (encryption, masking, tokenisation, consent) for each transmission path. | Security | P2 | Must | Data classification propagation is the most reliable mechanism to prevent accidental exposure of sensitive data fields. |
| REQ-SA14-004 | Data Ownership Assignment | Each data entity in the solution data model SHALL have an assigned data owner (role or team), and the suite SHALL surface ownership gaps (entities without an owner) as RAID issues, since unclear data ownership is a primary governance failure. | Governance | P2 | Should | Data ownership ambiguity causes access control, quality, and regulatory accountability gaps. |
| REQ-SA14-005 | Data Sovereignty Constraints | The suite SHALL capture data sovereignty requirements per data classification (e.g., personally identifiable information must reside in EU regions) and SHALL validate the deployment architecture and data replication design against these constraints, flagging violations. | Compliance | P2 | Must | Data sovereignty violations are a direct regulatory risk (GDPR Article 46, PDPA, etc.); automated checking at the SA stage prevents architecture designs that would require costly remediation. |
| REQ-SA14-006 | Master Data & Reference Data Identification | The suite SHALL identify entities in the solution data model that are likely master data (Customer, Product, Organisation) or reference data (Country, Currency, Status) and SHALL prompt the SA to confirm whether the solution will own, consume, or replicate this data, generating integration requirements accordingly. | Functional | P2 | Should | Master data governance decisions at the SA stage prevent duplicate master records, data inconsistency, and expensive MDM remediation programmes. |
| REQ-SA14-007 | Data Architecture Handover to Data Architect | The solution data design SHALL be exportable as a structured handover artefact to the Data Architect role (V05), including: all data stores, entities, flows, classifications, ownership, sovereignty constraints, and outstanding design decisions requiring Data Architect input. | Integration | P2, P7 | Must | Clear SA-to-Data-Architect handover prevents architectural decisions about data persistence being made ad hoc by development teams. |

## SA-15 — RAID Register Management {#sa-15}

### SA-15-A: RAID Capture & Lifecycle

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA15-001 | Unified RAID Register | The suite SHALL maintain a unified RAID (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies) register per solution, with each entry capturing: Type (Risk/Assumption/Issue/Dependency), ID (auto-generated), Title, Description, Category (Technical/Delivery/Organisational/Regulatory/Commercial/Security), Status, Owner, Date Raised, Target Resolution Date, Likelihood (1–5), Impact (1–5), Risk Score (Likelihood × Impact), and Mitigation/Action. | Data | P2 | Must | RAID registers are a universal project management and architecture governance tool; their absence means risks and dependencies are managed informally in email, leading to surprises. |
| REQ-SA15-002 | AI-Assisted RAID Population | The AI engine SHALL analyse the solution brief, requirements, option analysis, ADRs, NFR register, integration inventory, and data design and SHALL automatically propose RAID entries with populated fields, for SA review and acceptance or rejection. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | AI-assisted RAID generation from structured solution content ensures comprehensive initial population and catches risks that SAs might overlook. |
| REQ-SA15-003 | Risk Heat Map | The suite SHALL render a risk heat map (likelihood vs. impact matrix) visualising all open risks, colour-coded by score (Low/Medium/High/Critical), with click-through to each risk's full record. | UX | P2 | Must | Risk heat maps are the most widely used format for communicating risk status to stakeholders and governance bodies. |
| REQ-SA15-004 | RAID Linkage to ADRs and Requirements | RAID entries SHALL be linkable to ADRs (decisions made to mitigate a risk), requirements (requirements that introduce or constrain a risk), and RAID entries in other solutions (for programme-level dependency tracking). | Functional | P2 | Should | RAID entries without linkage are isolated; linked entries enable impact analysis when a linked artefact changes. |
| REQ-SA15-005 | Dependency Register with Owner Confirmation | All Dependencies SHALL be tracked with: the depended-upon system or team, expected delivery date, confirmed vs. unconfirmed status, and the consequence of the dependency being late or unavailable; unconfirmed dependencies past their expected date SHALL trigger automatic alerts to the SA and dependency owner. | Functional | P2 | Must | Unconfirmed dependencies are the leading cause of delivery schedule failures; automated alerting drives resolution before critical path impact. |
| REQ-SA15-006 | RAID Status Lifecycle | Each RAID entry SHALL follow a status lifecycle appropriate to its type: Risks (Open / Mitigated / Accepted / Closed); Assumptions (Unvalidated / Validated / Refuted); Issues (Open / In Progress / Resolved / Closed); Dependencies (Unconfirmed / Confirmed / Delivered / Blocked). | Governance | P2 | Must | Status-less RAID entries cannot be managed; lifecycle states drive actions and allow staleness identification. |
| REQ-SA15-007 | RAID Review Cadence | The suite SHALL support scheduled RAID review cycles (weekly, fortnightly, monthly), generating a RAID review summary report (new entries, changed status, overdue mitigations, escalation recommendations) distributable to the SA and sponsors. | Reporting | P2 | Should | Regular RAID reviews prevent registers from becoming stale; automated summaries reduce the overhead of preparing review materials. |
| REQ-SA15-008 | RAID Export | The RAID register SHALL be exportable to Excel/CSV for programme-level consolidation, and to Word/PDF for governance gate packs, formatted per the V10 deliverable templates. | Reporting | P2 | Must | RAID registers are regularly requested in programme reporting; manual extraction and formatting is a friction point. |
| REQ-SA15-009 | Programme-Level RAID Aggregation | Where multiple solutions are part of a programme, the suite SHALL aggregate their RAID registers into a programme-level view, deduplicated and cross-linked, allowing programme managers to identify shared risks and dependencies. | Reporting | P1, P2 | Should | Programme risk management requires a consolidated cross-solution RAID view; solution-level silos hide shared risks from programme governance. |

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA16-001 | Solution Roadmap Builder | The suite SHALL provide a solution roadmap builder that allows the SA to define phases/releases/milestones on a timeline, assign capabilities and requirements to phases, show predecessor/successor dependencies between phases, and annotate milestones with governance gate events. | Functional | P2 | Must | Solution roadmaps communicate the delivery sequencing of architectural capabilities and are required for stakeholder alignment and resource planning. |
| REQ-SA16-002 | Transition Architecture States | The suite SHALL support the definition of named transition architecture states (current state → state N → target state), each capturing a snapshot of: active components, integrations, data stores, and technology stack; transition states SHALL be rendered as separate architecture views. | Functional | P2 | Must | Transition architectures are a TOGAF ADM Phase E/F deliverable; they are essential for communicating co-existence and migration complexity in incremental delivery. |

| REQ-SA16-004 | Gap Analysis Generation | The suite SHALL compute and display a gap analysis between the current state architecture and each target/transition state, listing components to be added, modified, decommissioned, and integrated, with each gap linked to delivery requirements. | AI/ML | P2 | Must | Gap analysis is the bridge between current and target states; automated generation from structured state definitions eliminates manual comparison. |
| REQ-SA16-005 | Migration / Cutover Approach | The suite SHALL support capture and documentation of migration approach options (big bang, phased, strangler fig, parallel run) with pros/cons for each in the context of the solution, the recommended approach, and a high-level migration plan. | Functional | P2 | Must | Migration strategy is a critical SA decision that directly impacts delivery risk and business continuity; it must be documented before delivery begins. |
| REQ-SA16-006 | Decommission Planning | Where the solution replaces an existing system, the suite SHALL generate a decommission plan capturing: decommission prerequisites (data migration verified, all consumers migrated), decommission timeline, data retention policy post-decommission, rollback conditions, and responsible parties. | Functional | P2 | Should | Decommission planning is consistently deferred and then poorly executed; capturing it at the SA stage ensures it is resourced and scheduled. |
| REQ-SA16-007 | Roadmap–Portfolio Linkage | The solution roadmap SHALL be linkable to the enterprise portfolio (V09), allowing enterprise architects to see aggregate delivery timelines across solutions competing for shared resources. | Integration | P1, P2 | Should | Solution roadmaps disconnected from portfolio create resource contention surprises; linked roadmaps enable proactive capacity planning at the programme/portfolio level. |
| REQ-SA16-008 | Roadmap Visualisation | The suite SHALL render the solution roadmap as: (a) a swimlane Gantt view (phases × time), (b) a capability increments view (capability enabled per phase), and (c) a transition state timeline view; all views SHALL be exportable as image and embeddable in Word/PPT deliverables. | UX | P2 | Should | Multiple roadmap visualisations serve different audiences (executives, delivery teams, capability owners); export and embed enables reuse in governance communications. |

## SA-17 — Solution Review, Governance Gates & Quality Gates {#sa-17}

### SA-17-A: Review & Gate Management

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| REQ-SA17-001 | Configurable Gate Framework | The suite SHALL allow enterprises to configure a solution governance gate framework defining: gate names (e.g., Solution Brief Approval / HLD Review / LLD Review / Architecture Review Board / Pre-Implementation Gate / Post-Implementation Review), gate sequence, mandatory artefacts required at each gate, mandatory approvers, and pass/fail criteria. | Governance | P2 | Must | Architecture governance gate definitions vary by organisation; a configurable framework allows the suite to enforce whichever process is in use without requiring custom development. |
| REQ-SA17-002 | Gate Readiness Check | Before a solution is submitted to a gate the suite SHALL automatically check completeness of all mandatory artefacts (brief approved, HLD sections complete, all ADRs accepted or justified, NFR register complete, RAID register populated, key diagrams generated), compute a readiness score, and list outstanding items the SA must resolve before submission. | Governance | P2 | Must | Gate readiness checks prevent the wasted effort of submitting incomplete packages for review, which delays decisions and consumes reviewer time. |
| REQ-SA17-003 | Architecture Review Board (ARB) Package Generation | The suite SHALL auto-generate an ARB review package (Word/PDF/PPT) containing: executive summary, scope boundary, key decisions (ADRs), HLD overview, NFR targets, RAID summary, technology selections, and open issues, formatted per the V10 enterprise deliverable templates. | Reporting | P2 | Must | ARB packages are a high-effort SA activity; AI generation from structured solution data reduces preparation time from days to hours. |
| REQ-SA17-004 | Gate Decision Capture | ARB or gate reviewer SHALL be able to record a gate decision (Approved / Approved with Conditions / Deferred / Rejected) with comments and required actions; conditions and actions SHALL automatically generate RAID entries assigned to the SA. | Governance | P2 | Must | Gate decisions without captured conditions and actions are verbal-only, leading to conditions not being tracked or resolved. |
| REQ-SA17-005 | Design Review Checklist Library | The suite SHALL provide a library of design review checklists for each solution type and SA sub-discipline (security design review, data design review, integration design review, infrastructure design review, API design review), each configurable to add or remove criteria. | Governance | P2 | Must | Design review checklists are the most consistent mechanism for ensuring quality across solutions reviewed by different individuals; a library prevents checklist reinvention per project. |
| REQ-SA17-006 | Peer Review Workflow | The suite SHALL support a peer review workflow for SA artefacts (HLD, LLD, ADRs) where the SA nominates peers, peers receive notifications and can comment on or approve specific sections, and the SA must resolve all unaddressed peer comments before gate submission. | Governance | P2 | Should | Peer reviews are the most effective quality gate for design artefacts; structured workflows improve review completion rates. |
| REQ-SA17-007 | Post-Implementation Review (PIR) Template | After solution go-live the suite SHALL generate a PIR template pre-populated with: original NFR targets vs. measured production metrics, original cost estimate vs. actual cost, delivered vs. planned scope, ADRs validated or invalidated by production behaviour, and lessons learned prompts. | Reporting | P2 | Should | PIRs close the SA feedback loop; their absence means architecture errors are repeated and SA estimation models never improve. |
| REQ-SA17-008 | Quality Gate Metric Dashboard | The suite SHALL provide a quality gate metrics dashboard showing across all solutions: time-at-each-gate, gate pass rate (first time vs. conditional vs. rejected), most common gate failure reasons, and average time to resolve gate conditions, enabling continuous improvement of the SA process. | Reporting | P1, P2 | Could | Process metrics drive improvement; without them, gate quality is anecdotal and systematic defects are not identified. |

| ID | Title | SHALL Description | Category | Persona(s) | Priority | Rationale/Source |
|----|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|

| REQ-SA18-004 | Enterprise Architecture Standards Compliance Report | Before handover the suite SHALL generate an Enterprise Architecture Standards Compliance Report mapping every significant technology choice, pattern, and design decision to the relevant enterprise standard or principle (from the V01 EA layer), with status (Compliant / Deviation / Waiver Requested / Not Applicable) for each. | Governance | P1, P2 | Must | Compliance reports are required by EA governance bodies to confirm that solutions are architecturally aligned before delivery funding is released. |

| REQ-SA18-006 | EA Repository Publication | On handover approval the suite SHALL publish the solution architecture to the EA architecture repository (V01 layer), creating or updating the solution view in the enterprise landscape with: components, integrations, data stores, technology stack, and linkage to business capabilities. | Integration | P1, P2 | Must | Without EA repository publication, the enterprise architecture landscape is not maintained and EA governance cannot see the impact of new solutions. |
| REQ-SA18-007 | Architect of Record Assignment | The solution SHALL have a designated Architect of Record (AoR) field, who is the accountable named architect throughout delivery and operations; the AoR SHALL receive notifications for any architecture-impacting changes raised during delivery. | Governance | P2 | Must | "Orphaned" solutions without a named architect of record lead to ad hoc delivery decisions that deviate from the approved architecture without notice. |
| REQ-SA18-008 | Architecture Debt Registration | Any known architecture debt accepted at handover (deferred quality, temporary workarounds, acknowledged NFR shortfalls) SHALL be registered in the Architecture Debt Register (linked to the EA layer V01 or the solution RAID register) with: description, debt amount (estimated remediation cost/effort), interest rate (cost of leaving it unremediated per release), and target resolution phase. | Governance | P1, P2 | Should | Architecture debt not registered at handover is forgotten; a debt register drives prioritisation of remediation in future roadmap phases. |
| REQ-SA18-009 | Continuous Architecture Support Model | The suite SHALL support a Continuous Architecture support model during delivery by maintaining: the solution workspace in an active state, allowing ADR creation for in-flight decisions, RAID updates, and HLD/LLD amendment through a change request workflow, with full audit trail. | Functional | P2 | Should | Architecture finishes at HLD/LLD sign-off only on paper; in practice, SAs make decisions throughout delivery; the suite must support this reality. |
