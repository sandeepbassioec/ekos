AHMED HASSAN [00:03]:
Good news — the offline ping buffer is genuinely lightweight. I built a proof of concept
in 1.5 days. Here's what it does: when the device loses connectivity, the app stores
GPS pings in AsyncStorage with timestamps. Max buffer size: 50 pings (25 minutes of
Travelling pings, or 4+ hours of In Progress pings). When connectivity restores, the
buffer flushes to POST /api/v2/location/ping with a "replayed: true" flag and the
original clientTimestamp. The server processes them in chronological order.

The server-side change is minimal: the ping endpoint needs to accept clientTimestamp
as a separate field from server receipt time, and store it in technician_locations.
Total estimate: 3 days mobile + 1 day backend. That's 4 days total.

PRIYA NAIR [00:07]:
That's really good. 4 days is totally absorb-able. Daniel, this directly responds to
the Region East pilot feedback. What do you think?

DANIEL FROST [00:08]:
I think we have to do it. The pilot showed 23 of 40 technicians affected. If we go live
without it, we'll have SLA alerts firing for technicians in tunnels or buildings — our
dispatchers will lose trust in the dashboard within a week.

PRIYA NAIR [00:10]:
Agreed. I'm making a formal decision here: offline ping buffer is IN scope for v2.0.
